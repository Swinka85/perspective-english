ABOUT THIS LESSON

About This Longevity ESL Lesson

Scientists are studying aging itself as a process that might one day be slowed. The aim is not simply to extend lifespan, but to give people more healthy years and delay diseases associated with old age.

This B1–B2 lesson explores the benefits and consequences of longer lives. Students consider access to expensive treatments, pressure on housing and pensions, retirement and the ethical question of whether an ending helps give life meaning.

Students explore what thirty extra years could mean for work, family and relationships before acting as government health advisers and deciding which treatment deserves funding.

FROM THE ARTICLE

Imagine reaching the age of 100 but still feeling forty. For most of history this was a fairy tale, yet in laboratories around the world scientists are now studying aging itself, not as something inevitable, but as a process that might one day be slowed.
