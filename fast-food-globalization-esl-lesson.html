LESSON PREVIEW

ABOUT THIS LESSON

Fast food is one of the clearest symbols of globalization. The same familiar brands can appear in Seoul, London, Tokyo, Mumbai, and New York, giving travelers a taste of something they already know even when everything else feels unfamiliar.

But global food is rarely identical everywhere. Chains change their menus to match local tastes, ingredients, religions, and eating habits. At the same time, foods such as Korean fried chicken, sushi, curry, pizza, and bubble tea cross borders and change again as new cultures make them their own.

This magazine-style English lesson explores fast food, globalization, localization, food identity, global brands, and cultural exchange. Students read an original article and take part in vocabulary, discussion, critical-thinking, speaking, and writing activities about what happens when food travels across borders.

FROM THE ARTICLE

Fast Food, Slow Change began as an advertisement. More than a century later, anonymous inspectors can change a restaurant's future with a single star. But when a simple bowl of noodles can be unforgettable and an expensive meal can disappoint, can anyone really decide what the world's best food is?
