ABOUT THIS LESSON

About This Public Etiquette ESL Lesson

Nobody hands us a rulebook for public life, yet most of us quickly notice when somebody breaks one of its unwritten rules. This B1–B2 public etiquette ESL lesson explores the social norms that shape how we behave around strangers, from talking on public transportation and giving up seats to personal space, escalators and eating in public.

Students examine how ideas about polite behavior can change between countries, cities, generations and individuals. The lesson uses examples from places including South Korea, Japan, the UK and the United States to show how behavior that feels completely normal in one place may be considered rude or uncomfortable somewhere else.

Rather than teaching a fixed list of correct manners, the lesson asks a more interesting question: who should adapt when two different cultural rulebooks meet? Students debate whether visitors should follow local expectations, whether locals should tolerate differences and how new technology is creating public behaviors before society has agreed on the rules.

FROM THE ARTICLE

Public life runs on an unwritten code. We learn it young, absorb it without noticing, and then mistake it for simple good manners. Are people breaking the rules, or just following a different rulebook?
