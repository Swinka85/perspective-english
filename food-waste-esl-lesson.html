ABOUT THIS LESSON

About This Food Waste ESL Lesson

Every day, enormous amounts of perfectly edible food are thrown away by households, restaurants, supermarkets and businesses. What looks like a small amount of waste in one kitchen becomes a major global problem when repeated across millions of homes.

This free B1–B2 Food Waste ESL lesson explores why so much food is wasted and the environmental consequences of producing food that nobody eats. Students consider everyday habits, sustainable consumption and practical ways individuals, businesses and governments could reduce food waste.

The lesson combines an original reading and audio with vocabulary, comprehension, discussion questions and critical-thinking activities. Students use English to discuss food waste, sustainability, consumption, environmental impact and what could realistically be done to waste less food.

FROM THE ARTICLE

Every evening, in kitchens and restaurants around the world, a quiet waste takes place. Perfectly good bread, bruised fruit, and half-eaten meals are scraped into the bin without a second thought. It feels harmless, but multiplied across billions of homes, it becomes one of the planet’s largest and most overlooked problems. Roughly a third of all the food grown for people is never eaten.
