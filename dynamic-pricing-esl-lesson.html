ABOUT THIS LESSON

About This Dynamic Pricing ESL Lesson

Two passengers can board the same flight, sit only a few rows apart and arrive at exactly the same time, yet one may have paid hundreds of dollars more than the other. This B1–B2 ESL lesson explores dynamic pricing and asks a deceptively simple question: what is something actually worth?

Students examine how airlines, hotels, ride-hailing apps, concert promoters and other businesses change prices according to timing, demand and availability. The article considers the economic argument for flexible prices while also exploring the less comfortable role of algorithms and the possibility of businesses estimating how much an individual customer is willing to pay.

Students then connect the topic to their own experiences through discussion, run a music festival in the You Set the Price critical-thinking task, and defend their pricing decisions against an angry customer and journalist before writing their own response to the lesson's central question.

FROM THE ARTICLE

If a price depends on who is buying, when they buy and how badly they want it, what is something actually worth?
