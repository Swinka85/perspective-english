TWO WAYS TO TEACH EACH LESSON

60-minute class

One class a week, or when time is tight.

  1. 10 minWarm-up question, then the article with the audio
  2. 10 minVocabulary and true-or-false check
  3. 15 minCritical-thinking activity in pairs or small groups
  4. 20 minSpeaking challenge: groups prepare and a few present
  5. 5 minWrap-up: one discussion question as a class

Homework: Language in Context and the 200–250 word writing task.

150-minute class

Two 75-minute classes, or one long block.

  1. 10 minWarm-up with the first two discussion questions
  2. 20 minArticle with the audio, read twice
  3. 15 minVocabulary and true-or-false check
  4. 20 minDiscussion questions in groups, then report back
  5. 15 minLanguage in Context
  6. 25 minCritical-thinking activity, then compare as a class
  7. 35 minSpeaking challenge: every group prepares, presents and the class votes
  8. 10 minPlan the writing task together

Homework: finish the 200–250 word writing task.

MAKING IT WORK

Same routine every week. Every Perspective lesson follows the same format, so by week 3 students know exactly what to expect and you can prepare a class in a few minutes.

Swap freely. Each unit lists spare lessons on the same theme. If a topic won’t land with your group, swap it and the unit still holds together.

Big classes take longer. The timings assume a class of around 20. With 30 or more students, give the speaking challenge extra time or have only some groups present each week.

Practice outside class. Every lesson has a free online Practice Room. Students can go over the vocabulary and activities on their phones before the exam weeks.
