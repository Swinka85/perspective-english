ISSUE 029

The Attention Economy

Who is really paying when everything is free?

A ready-to-teach English lesson exploring the attention economy, smartphones, social media, algorithms, distraction, and why our attention has become so valuable.

Buy Lesson Listen to Audio

LESSON PREVIEW

ABOUT THIS LESSON

Many of the world's most popular apps cost nothing to download. Social media, videos, games, news, and entertainment can all appear to be free. But there is something extremely valuable that users give these platforms every day: their attention.

In the attention economy, companies compete for people's limited time. Notifications, infinite scrolling, recommendations, autoplay, and personalized feeds are designed to keep users watching, clicking, and returning for more.

This magazine-style English lesson explores why attention has become a valuable resource, how technology competes for our time, and whether we are really choosing how we spend it. Students read an original article and take part in vocabulary, discussion, critical-thinking, speaking, and writing activities.

FROM THE ARTICLE

Every day, billions of people open apps that cost nothing. They scroll through videos, check messages, read news, and watch entertainment without paying a cent. But these services are not really free. Instead of paying with money, users pay with something much more limited: their attention.
