ESL Articles for Discussion

Give students something worth talking about before asking them to talk. These B1–B2 ESL articles combine accessible reading with vocabulary, comprehension, critical thinking and discussion around modern topics that can produce more than one reasonable point of view.

Strong classroom discussion usually starts before the first question is asked. When every student has read the same short article, the class has shared information, useful vocabulary and a common starting point. That makes it easier for learners to explain an opinion, challenge an idea and respond to classmates instead of relying only on personal experience.

The ESL articles for discussion below are designed primarily for B1–B2 adult and university learners. Each Perspective lesson begins with an original article and then moves into vocabulary, comprehension, discussion and critical-thinking activities. Audio, teacher support and classroom presentation materials are also included with each complete lesson.

The aim is not simply to check whether students understood a text. The reading gives them enough background to make a decision, compare perspectives, solve a problem or debate a question that does not have one obvious answer.

How to use an ESL article for discussion

Start by giving students a reason to read. A prediction question, image or simple choice before the article helps them approach the text with a purpose. Avoid explaining every difficult word in advance. Choose the vocabulary students actually need to understand the argument and participate in the discussion.

After reading, use comprehension questions to establish the facts, but do not let the lesson stop there. Move from What did the article say? to What do you think should happen? The strongest discussion questions usually involve a decision, trade-off or disagreement rather than asking students simply whether they agree.

For quieter groups, give students one or two minutes to prepare an opinion and a reason before they speak. You can also give the conversation an outcome: reach a group decision, rank possible solutions, create a policy, choose the strongest argument or defend a position. A clear task makes students listen to one another instead of taking turns answering isolated questions.

Why B1–B2 works well for article-based discussion

Intermediate learners are often able to express opinions but may not have enough background knowledge or topic vocabulary to sustain a longer discussion. A short, accessible article solves both problems. It gives everyone the same information and introduces language that students can reuse immediately.

The topic still matters. Articles work best when students can connect the issue to modern life and when more than one position can be defended. Technology, work, media, consumer behavior, culture and environmental choices are especially useful because students can combine information from the text with their own experience.
