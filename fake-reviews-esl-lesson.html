Fake Reviews

Can you trust what you read online?

LESSON PREVIEW

ABOUT THIS LESSON

Before choosing a restaurant, booking a hotel, or buying a new product, millions of people do the same thing: they check the reviews. A high star rating can make an unknown business look trustworthy, while a few terrible reviews can make customers stay away.

But online reviews are not always what they seem. Businesses can pay for positive reviews, competitors can post negative ones, and fake accounts can create the appearance of genuine customer experiences.

This magazine-style English lesson explores fake reviews, online trust, consumer behavior, and how difficult it can be to separate genuine opinions from manipulation. Students read an original article and take part in vocabulary, discussion, critical-thinking, speaking, and writing activities.

FROM THE ARTICLE

Before booking a hotel, choosing a restaurant, or buying a new pair of headphones, many of us do the same thing: check the reviews. A few glowing five-star ratings can turn an unknown product into an instant favorite. But what happens when the people writing those reviews never bought the product at all?
