ABOUT THIS LESSON

About This Superstitions ESL Lesson

This advanced C1–C2 lesson examines how the same signs can be interpreted as good or bad luck in different cultures, and why superstitions persist even among highly educated people.

Students analyze examples involving black cats, unlucky numbers, wedding rain, owls and gifts. They explore five advanced expressions, infer meaning from the article and discuss how culture, memory and uncertainty shape beliefs.

Groups evaluate six opposing omens, defend which rituals could influence their behavior, design an international hotel policy under challenge and write a 200–250 word argument addressing the strongest opposing view.

FROM THE ARTICLE

A black cat can signal disaster in one country and good fortune in another. A small ritual before an exam may calm nerves and even become a self-fulfilling prophecy. Cultural meanings can be ingrained long before we question them.
