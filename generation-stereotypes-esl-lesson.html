LESSON PREVIEW

ABOUT THIS LESSON

Baby Boomers, Generation X, Millennials, and Gen Z are often described as if each generation has its own personality. One group is called hardworking, another entitled, another anxious or obsessed with technology. These labels can feel convincing because we hear them everywhere.

But people born in the same period can have very different lives, values, and experiences. Age matters, yet so do culture, economics, technology, family, and individual personality. Generational labels can help us discuss social change, but they can also turn complicated people into simple stereotypes.

This magazine-style English lesson explores generational stereotypes, identity, age labels, the workplace, technology, and the generation gap. Students read an original article and take part in vocabulary, discussion, critical-thinking, speaking, and writing activities about whether generations are really as different as we think.

FROM THE ARTICLE

The Generation Myth began as an advertisement. More than a century later, anonymous inspectors can change a restaurant's future with a single star. But when a simple bowl of noodles can be unforgettable and an expensive meal can disappoint, can anyone really decide what the world's best food is?
