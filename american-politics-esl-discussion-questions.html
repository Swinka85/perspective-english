American Politics ESL Discussion Questions: Red vs. Blue

15 classroom conversation questions about America’s two big parties, political identity, polarization, and how people with different views get along.
A1–A2 BeginnerB1–B2 IntermediateC1–C2 Advanced

What divides America’s two big parties, and can people on opposite sides still stay close? Explore party colors and symbols, the two-party system, and political disagreement with 15 ESL discussion questions from Perspective English Issue 056, “Red vs. Blue.” The questions focus on ideas, not people, and nobody has to say how they vote. Use these level-specific questions for pair work, group discussion, and classroom debates.

A

Beginner · A1–A2

Start the conversation

  1. 01

    What colors or symbols do parties use in your country?

  2. 02

    Have you ever argued with a friend about politics? What happened?

  3. 03

    Why do some people watch only news that agrees with them?

  4. 04

    Can two people be close friends if they vote for different parties?

  5. 05

    Is it better for a country to have two big parties, or many small ones?

B

Intermediate · B1–B2

Explore the issue

  1. 01

    Do you ever talk about politics with friends or family? How does it usually go?

  2. 02

    Have you ever supported a group or idea even though you disagreed with part of it?

  3. 03

    Why do people sometimes support a party even when they disagree with some of its policies?

  4. 04

    Should political opinions affect personal relationships, or can opposite sides stay close friends?

  5. 05

    Is having two major political parties better than having several?

C

Advanced · C1–C2

Challenge the idea

  1. 01

    Would you feel comfortable discussing politics with someone who votes differently?

  2. 02

    Have you ever changed your mind on a political issue? What changed it?

  3. 03

    Why might disliking the other side come more easily than disagreeing with it?

  4. 04

    Does a winner-takes-all system help or harm a divided country?

  5. 05

    If both sides misjudge each other, whose job is it to close the gap?
