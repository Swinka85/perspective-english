Memories for Sale

Are experiences really worth more than things?

A ready-to-teach English lesson exploring experiences versus possessions, happiness, travel, concerts, social media, status, authenticity, and what makes something worth paying for.

LESSON PREVIEW

ABOUT THIS LESSON

For generations, success was often measured by the things people owned: a home, a car, fashionable clothes, electronics, and other visible signs of wealth. Today, many consumers say they would rather spend their money on experiences.

Travel, concerts, restaurants, festivals, wellness retreats, and once-in-a-lifetime activities can create memories that last for years. But as businesses learn how to package and sell those moments, experiences can start to look a lot like the consumer products people once wanted to escape.

This magazine-style English lesson explores experiences versus possessions, happiness, status, social media, authenticity, and consumer culture. Students read an original article and take part in vocabulary, discussion, critical-thinking, speaking, and writing activities about what makes something worth paying for.

FROM THE ARTICLE

Memories for Sale began as an advertisement. More than a century later, anonymous inspectors can change a restaurant's future with a single star. But when a simple bowl of noodles can be unforgettable and an expensive meal can disappoint, can anyone really decide what the world's best food is?
