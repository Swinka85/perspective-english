ABOUT THIS LESSON

About This Four-Day Work Week ESL Lesson

The five-day working week feels normal, but a growing number of workplaces have experimented with giving employees the same pay for fewer working days.

This B1–B2 lesson explores whether working less could improve productivity and wellbeing. Students consider evidence from four-day week trials while also examining practical problems for hospitals, shops, factories and other workplaces that cannot simply close for an extra day.

Students decide which workers could realistically receive a four-day week, prepare a case to persuade a manager to try it, and finish by giving a balanced written opinion.

FROM THE ARTICLE

The idea is not about doing less work in more time, but about protecting wellbeing without losing productivity. Long hours are often linked to stress and burnout, which can make people slower and less creative.
