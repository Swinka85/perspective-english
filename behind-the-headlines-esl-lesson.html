ABOUT THIS LESSON

About This Media Bias ESL Lesson

Two news organizations can report the same event and leave their audiences with very different impressions. Headlines, photographs, quotations and the details journalists choose to include can all influence how a story is understood.

This B1–B2 Media Bias ESL lesson explores news headlines, framing, clickbait and editorial choices. Students examine how news organizations can present the same facts differently and consider how language and presentation influence an audience.

The lesson combines an original news and media reading with audio, vocabulary, comprehension, discussion questions and critical-thinking activities. Students use English to discuss media bias, trust in news, headlines, journalism and how to become more critical consumers of information.

FROM THE ARTICLE

Two news organizations can report the same event and leave their audiences with completely different impressions. The facts may be similar, but the headline, photograph, quotations, and missing details can quietly change the story.
