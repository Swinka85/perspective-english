ABOUT THIS LESSON

About This UK ESL Lesson

This advanced C1–C2 lesson examines why England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland are distinct countries within one sovereign state, and why nationality and identity cannot always be reduced to a passport.

Students explore the Welsh language, devolved governments, national sports teams and competing ideas of belonging. They analyze five sophisticated expressions, infer meaning from the article and debate conflicting definitions of a country.

Groups select four criteria for nationhood, apply them consistently to the four UK countries, defend a tourist-board pitch under challenge and write a 200–250 word argument addressing an opposing view.

FROM THE ARTICLE

The United Kingdom is one sovereign state containing four countries. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have devolved governments, while England does not. Sport, language and personal identity complicate the picture: citizenship and belonging are related, but not the same thing.
