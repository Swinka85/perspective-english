ABOUT THIS LESSON

About This Michelin Guide ESL Lesson

For more than a century, the Michelin Guide has influenced where people eat and which restaurants are considered among the best in the world. Yet its story began not with chefs, but with a tire company trying to encourage people to drive more.

This B1–B2 Michelin Guide ESL lesson explores how anonymous Michelin inspectors award restaurant stars and how those ratings can transform a restaurant's reputation. Students consider the pressure Michelin stars create for chefs and whether taste, quality and great food can ever be judged objectively.

The lesson combines an original reading and audio with vocabulary, comprehension, discussion questions and critical-thinking activities. Students use English to discuss Michelin stars, restaurant ratings, fine dining, taste, price, prestige, tradition and food culture.

FROM THE ARTICLE

The Michelin Guide began as an advertisement. More than a century later, anonymous inspectors can change a restaurant's future with a single star. But when a simple bowl of noodles can be unforgettable and an expensive meal can disappoint, can anyone really decide what the world's best food is?
